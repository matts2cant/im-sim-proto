# 130. Device overhaul: one design language, rack and desk versions, door controllers, frame status (M13c)

Status: Accepted (built in three steps; step 0, the 3D models in a hub showroom, waits for the user's approval of the look before the rest)

Date: 2026-11-13

## Context

After the M13 playtest the user found network gear hard to tell apart (routers, switches and the mail server were similar dark boxes on walls), some devices showing two lights, and the interaction frame useful enough to carry more information. The user and the chief engineer settled the overhaul in a design review (grilling, two rounds) and three mockups: [network_gear_looks.html](../prototypes/network_gear_looks.html) (the first look, the linking bay, the frame's status line) and [device_language.html](../prototypes/device_language.html) (every device in every version, the bays, the deck's Map and the keychain), approved with "no ports on small devices".

## Decision

**One design language for every device** ([device_language.html](../prototypes/device_language.html)):
- a strip in the device kind's colour (on a rack unit, its left ear; on a standalone device, along its top): switch blue, router orange, access point cyan, wall jack slate, computer green, alarm system red (controller, siren, radio base station, panic button), door controller violet, security yellow (camera, turret, laser);
- the kind's Map icon (`DeviceKinds`) on its front in that colour; the rack server uses the computer icon (the server icon is retired);
- one status light, none on wall jacks, panic buttons and lasers (their status shows in the frame);
- visible network ports on network gear (switches, routers, access points), computers, alarm controllers and wall jacks, each with a port light (lit while cabled and both ends up) and a label naming the connected device, running vertically under its socket (the wall jack's label sits flat under its socket); small single-purpose devices (camera, turret, siren, radio base station, panic button, laser, door controller) show no port;
- a name plate.
The deck's Map draws the same colour strip and icon on every node, with the status square in the light's colours.

**Devices come in versions with the same behaviour**:
- routers and switches: a rack unit (switch 1U, 24 sockets; router 2U, uplink + 8) or a small desk box (switch 5 sockets; router uplink + 4); wall-mounted routers and switches are retired; versions differ only in looks, and the sockets on the model are the most cables it takes (a level test checks it); desk gear is fixed in place;
- computers: desktop (today), rack server (2U; E opens its OS screen like a desktop) and later a laptop;
- alarm controllers: the wall panel (today) or a 2U rack unit.

**Linking bays**: a floor-standing rack (60 x 190 cm) holding the network gear of an area, cables bundled to the ceiling, a name plate on top. Furniture, not a device: each unit inside is powered and cabled on its own. Some bays are open; others have a glass door locked with a mechanical lock (key or lockpick). A closed door blocks reaching the units (their lights show through the glass; the frame on the door reads "Locked · key or lockpick").

**Servers are computers**: there is no mail server. The mail host logic and the M12c mail routes are removed; mail returns once the network is settled.

**Door controllers**: a door is not a network device; its controller on the wall beside it is (its keypad, card reader, status box or control box; the boxes on both faces are one controller device: one cable, one name, one status, one light, the lock light). A door is on a network only through a controller; doors without one are mechanical. A broken or unpowered controller follows the door's power rule (fail-secure stays locked, fail-safe unlocks).

**Keys and the keychain**: a key item opens the mechanical locks that name it (doors and bays); a lock opens with its key or by picking it. Keys go to a keychain in the Inventory tab (a list of the keys collected, where each was found), never into the grid: no space, cannot be dropped or thrown.

**The interaction frame** ([ADR 0129](0129-interaction-frame.md)):
- every device and every character gets the frame, within reach (2 m) only;
- its tag gets a status line: network devices Online / Cut off (blinking) / Disabled or Broken / No power in the status light's colours; powered things off any network Powered / No power; characters Unconscious / Dead (awake: the name only); locks what opens them ("Locked · keycard", "Locked · key or lockpick");
- the "E: ..." prompts are removed; when E fails, a short message says why ("Locked: needs a keycard", "Too heavy", "No room to put down").

**Levels**: the network lab gets a showroom (an open bay, a locked bay, desk gear); the security wing one bay (its switch, router and the rack alarm controller); the warehouse a locked bay in the office and a small switch on the office desk. Every wall-mounted router, switch and mail server moves into a bay, or onto a desk for small ones.

**Steps**: 0) the 3D models in the new language, shown in a showroom in the hub, for the user's approval; 1) door controllers, the frame changes, the removed lights, removing mail hosting; 2) in parallel, the device scenes from the approved models (bays with locks, rack and desk versions, rack servers, rack alarm controller, port lights and labels, the key item and keychain, the Map's strips and icons); 3) the levels.

## Alternatives considered

- **Gear on walls with only a colour band**: the first mockup; the user asked for bays and versions to tidy rooms.
- **A frame visible up to 8 m with dimmed brackets beyond reach**: the user wants the frame at interaction distance only.
- **A mail host app on servers**: deferred; removed until the network is settled.
- **A door as the network device (M12 to M13)**: the controller is what a cable reaches in real buildings and gives the light a sensible place.
- **Bays locked by keycards**: mechanical locks pair with the new key and the lockpicking minigame.
- **Port labels as numbers with a legend card**: the user chose vertical labels; their legibility is checked in the showroom.

## Consequences

- Device scenes are rebuilt from shared model parts (kind strip, icon, port row with labels, name plate); `DeviceKinds` gains a colour per kind.
- Door hacking, the Map's door entries and the door exploit item move to door controllers; levels add controllers to every networked door.
- The M12c mail route mechanic, its tests and the mail-route consequences in the warehouse are removed.
- ADR 0122 (lights) and 0129 (frame) get amendments in step 1.

Readable at the player's 80 degree field of view: the switch's labels from about 0.4 m at 1080 p (about 0.25 to 0.3 m at 648 p), the router's and the single-port devices' from about 0.5 m at 1080 p (0.3 m at 648 p). Name plates read from about 1 m. Readability up close is a playtest question (checklist "M13c showroom").
- **Second pass (user review of the showroom)**: the access point is a round white ceiling-style disc (20 cm, 3.5 cm tall: a flat rim and a gentle dome), a flat centre disc with the kind icon, a glowing status ring round it (`StatusLed` shape `RING`) and a thin cyan band round the rim (`KindStrip` shape `BAND`); no plate, antennas or visible port (its cable enters from the back). The siren's horn is replaced by a beacon (a dark base ring, a translucent red-orange cover over a dark core) on top of its box, unlit for now. Door controllers lost their name plates; the desktop and laptop lost their icons; the laptop's lid is as wide as its base.
- **Tests**: `tests/objects/devices/models/` (every model's size, strip colour, icon, light, ports and no coplanar faces; the port row; the icon mesh; the bay's slots, blanks and door) and `tests/levels/hub/device_showroom_test.gd`; `KitGeometry.coplanar_mesh_overlaps()` is the mesh version of the CSG check. ## As built (step 1b: the frame, E's messages, keys)

- **The frame** ([ADR 0129](0129-interaction-frame.md) amendment): within reach (2 m) on what E acts on, or on whatever under the crosshair has a `Device`, a `Powered` or `BodyParts` (`TargetStatus.shows()`); the tag adds a status line and a lock line, live. `ui/hud/target_status.gd` (`TargetStatus`: pure rules `qualifies()`, `network_word()`, `powered_word()`, `character_word()`, `text()`, `color()`, `blink_alpha()`; readers `word_of()`, `lock_line()`, `name_of()`), `ui/hud/target_frame.gd` / `.tscn`, styles in `ui/hud/hud_theme.tres`.
- **How the status is read** (by components only): a character (BodyParts) from its `state.dead` / `state.unconscious` tags; a Device is on a network when it has links (cables listed on either end, its access point's radio link, or wireless clients) or names an access point; its word is No power (`Device.is_powered()` false), else Broken (`Device.broken`, `state.broken` or a broken Breakable), else Disabled (`DeviceStatus.is_disabled()`), else Online when one of the network gear it is linked to is up (linked to no gear: one of its links is up), else Cut off. A Device off any network with a `Powered`, or a `Powered` alone: Powered / No power (`Powered.on`, `state.unpowered`) / Broken; a Device off any network without a Powered says only Broken (the door today: no status line). The network rule reproduces `StatusLight.state_of()` / `on_network()` / `links_of()` (`TargetStatus.network_links()`, `on_network()`) so the HUD does not depend on a light being attached; the two should become one helper once step 2 settles `objects/devices/`.
- **E's messages**: `Interactor.message` (the HUD shows it): `Usable.refusal(user)` after a failed use (default: `LockRules.needs_message()` of the entity's locked Lockable: "Locked: needs a keycard" / "a code" / "a key or a lockpick"), `Usable.unavailable_reason(user)` for an unavailable usable (default `Usable.state_reason()`: "Broken", "No power"), "Too heavy", "No room to put down". The HUD's prompt label is removed; `Interactor.prompt` stays as data (tests read it).
- **Keys and the keychain**: `Keychain` (`actors/common/keychain/`) on any character (the player has one), saveable; `ItemData.keychain` marks an item that goes there (`items/key/key.tres`, `key.tscn`; stack state `key_id`, `key_name`; `ItemPickup.found_text`); `PlayerInventory.pick_up()` / `give()` route it ("Key added to the keychain: <name>"); the Inventory tab's far-right column lists the keys (`ui/tab_interface/tabs/keychain_list.gd`). `MechanicalLockData.key_id`; `MechanicalLock.use_locked()` unlocks for a holder of the key (`lock.unlocked`, how `key`); `locked_reason()` is "key or lockpick" with a key id, else "lockpick". `Lockable.reasons(user)` lists the locks' reasons.
- **Not done here** (other steps): Keys grant no navigation passage (`passage_layers()`), unlike keycards. No level places a key yet (step 3). The key is not in `ItemCatalog` (it never enters the grid).
- **Tests**: `tests/ui/hud/target_status_test.gd` (rules and readers on entities built from components), `target_frame_status_test.gd` (a device Online / Cut off / Disabled / No power, a Powered prop, a character awake / unconscious / dead, reach in at 1.5 m and out at 3 m, "Too heavy", no prompt label, an unavailable usable's "No power"), `target_frame_lock_test.gd` (a mechanically locked door: the lock line, E without the key, E with it), `tests/actors/common/keychain/keychain_test.gd`, `tests/objects/locks/lock_rules_test.gd` (lock line and message), `player_carry_ghost_test.gd` ("No room to put down"). Review shots: `tests/ui/hud/fixtures/target_frame_shots.tscn` (`device_online`, `device_cut`, `lamp_off`, `guard`, `guard_dead`, `door_locked`, `keychain`).

## As built (step 1a: door controllers, mail hosting removed)

- **The controller is an entity of its own** under the door's `Hardware` node (`<door>/Hardware/Controller`): `objects/door/door_controller.tscn` (a Node3D with Tags `material.electronics`, `object.device`, `object.door_controller`; a StimulusReceiver with the shared `command_open` / `close` / `lock` / `unlock` receptrons; `Device`; `DeviceLamp`; `DoorController`). A level places one there (a tscn node under the instanced door's `Hardware`) to put the door on a network: its Device's address, cables, trust and its `Powered`. A door whose locks put a box on the wall and has no controller gets a default one, built by `DoorHardware` (a level-placed one wins; a default one built earlier goes). A door with a controller but no lock's box gets a plain control box (as `wall_control`). A door without a box has no controller and no Device: it is mechanical. Under `Hardware` because the door's entity is busy readying its children while `Door._ready()` builds the hardware (Godot refuses `add_child` there); the hardware node is not.
- **One device, both faces**: `DoorHardware` builds the wall boxes on both faces as the controller's children and places the controller at the front face's first box (its cable and AR line end there). Each box is a StaticBody3D on the door's layer with the box collision shape, the approved model (`Model`, an instance of `objects/devices/models/door_controller_*.tscn`), a `DoorControl` (E) and a `DevicePanel` (`objects/devices/device_panel.gd`): `Device.of(box)` answers the controller's Device (`Device.of()` follows a DevicePanel child) and the panel forwards the deck's tool interface, so the deck, the frame and exploit items reach the same device from either box. `DoorController.of(box)` works too. The lock light is every box model's `StatusLed` LED, registered as the controller's `DeviceLamp` meshes (one light, both faces; the door's own colours red / green / dark through `Door`, the network state over them). The boxes are 10 x 20 cm now (the models), stacked by `DoorLayout` as before.
- **Commands and power** (`DoorController`, by component only): `command.open` / `close` call `Door.command()`; `command.lock` / `unlock` the door's `Lockable` (`how: command`); `device_status()` gives the door's words (locked / unlocked, then open / closed). While the controller's Device is down (unpowered or broken) the door's entity carries `state.unpowered`, so `ElectronicLock`'s existing rule applies (fail-safe unlocks, fail-secure stays locked) and the lock light goes dark. The door no longer has a Device, `DeviceLamp` or command receptrons (`door.tscn`; the breakable door's receiver keeps only `take_damage`), nor the leaf's status LED marker. The controller's name is its own `display_name`, else the door's (its `display_name`, else its node name), set when bound.
- **Kind**: `DeviceKinds` kind **Door controller** (tag `object.door_controller`, violet, the door icon, group Doors, exploit `door`) replaces the door kind; the four door controller models name it. The Map and the Security app list the controller under the door's name; the door exploit item opens it.
- **The frame** (merged with step 1b): `Usable.lock_target()` is the Lockable E works on (default: the entity's); `DoorControl` returns its door's, so `TargetStatus.lock_line()` shows the door's lock line on a box and `DoorControl.refusal()` says "Locked: needs a keycard"; `DoorControl.target_name()` is the controller's name. The box's status line comes from the controller's Device (Online / Cut off / ...).
- **Mail hosting removed**: `MailServer`, `MailRoute` and `InternetLink` are gone (`NetworkRoute` stays for loss of signal); `Computer.documents()` returns the inbox, network or not; `Computer.mail_server` and the screen's route refresh are gone. `mail_server.tscn` is a plain networked device (MAIL-01 stays in the warehouse until step 3). The network lab's mail PC keeps its edge router; its sign and mails now say the mail is on the PC.
- **Levels** (no relayout): every door that was on a network got a placed controller with its Device data, `Powered` and cables moved to it: the network lab's ClosetDoor; the security wing's D4Electronic and VaultDoor; the warehouse's FrontGate, SecurityDoor, BridgeDoor and VaultDoor. Keypad, card reader and roller doors get default controllers (standalone, a maintenance port, as their doors had before). Saves of the old door Devices are skipped with a LevelSnapshot warning (prototype data).
- **Tests**: `tests/objects/door/door_controller_test.gd` (a controller built from components on a plain door: one Device from both boxes, status, commands blocked while down, the lock light's network states, power loss and breakage through the controller following fail-secure / fail-safe, no Device without a box), `tests/ui/hud/target_frame_door_controller_test.gd` (the frame on a box: Online / Cut off, the door's lock line, E without the card), `exploit_items_test.gd` (the door exploit on a controller from either box), `computer_rules_test.gd` (mail with no network), `door_network_test.gd` (commands through the controller), and every level and showcase test re-pointed to `<door>/Hardware/Controller`.

## As built (step 2: device scenes from the models)

- **The desktop on a wall shelf**: levels place `computer.tscn` on walls (origin on the wall at 1.45 m), so the desktop model stands on a 0.72 x 0.47 m shelf with two brackets, 0.45 m below the origin (a 1.0 m standing desk at the levels' heights). Step 3 can put desktops on desks and drop the shelf.
- **One light, driven** (`ModelFace`, `objects/devices/model_face.gd`, and `StatusLight`): a device whose entity holds a `DeviceModel` always gets a StatusLight, which places nothing: it paints the model's `StatusLed` (online green, cut off amber blinking, disabled or broken red, unpowered dark) unless the entity's `DeviceLamp` lends it (the camera's lamp, the door controllers' lock lights, which keep the owner's colours through DeviceLamp as before). **A model without a StatusLed shows no light**: that is the rule for wall jacks, panic buttons and lasers (no `status_light` switch per scene). Model devices get no `GearPlate`: their `NamePlate`s show the device's display name (`NetworkSource.display_name()`). Devices without a model keep the placed LED, port lights and plate (DeviceSurface, markers) until step 3.
- **Ports**: a model's `PortRow` sockets are the device's ports. Its cabled links (`DeviceNetwork.cabled()`, both ways) take the sockets in cable order; a cable to a router takes a ringed uplink socket first (`ModelFace.socket_order()`). Each taken socket's label is the far end's display name (fitted to the row's `label_room`: shrunk to 70 % of its height, then cut), its port light lit while the device and the far end are both up. A model without a PortRow takes one cable through its back (`HIDDEN_SOCKETS`: the access point, camera, siren...). More cables than sockets is a level data error: `ModelFace.excess()` / `cable_error()` (pure), tested over every device scene (`tests/objects/devices/device_scenes_test.gd`); step 3 adds the level-wide test.
- **One network-state rule**: `NetworkState` (`objects/devices/network_state.gd`) holds `links_of()`, `is_networked()`, `on_network()` and `state_of()`; StatusLight, the frame's `TargetStatus` (its copy is gone) and the Map's `NetworkRow` read it. `StatusLight.State` and `COLORS` stay where they were.
- **Names in the frame**: a network device goes by its network name everywhere (`Interactor.display_name_of()` and `TargetStatus.name_of()` use `NetworkSource.display_name()` for any entity with a Device, before a usable's own label): the frame, the Map and the plate agree ("SW-OFFICE").
- **Siren beacon**: `BeaconGlow` (`objects/alarm/beacon_glow.gd`, generic) is the siren's `beacon_path`: shown while it sounds, it holds an emissive cover over the model's and a small red-orange OmniLight (3 m), and turns the beacon's reflector while visible; hidden, nothing runs per frame. Broken, the whole beacon hides.
- **Linking bays**: `objects/devices/models/linking_bay.tscn` is the open bay (furniture); its body is now open at the front (sides, top, plinth, back to the rails), so the Interactor's ray reaches the units, and device scenes on rack models snap to its slots like bare models (`LinkingBay.rack_units_of()`). `objects/devices/linking_bay_locked.tscn` adds a working glass door: a `GlassDoor` StaticBody3D (world layer) hinged on the bay's left edge with `Lockable` (locked), a `MechanicalLock` (4 pins; the key id is left for step 3) and a new generic component **`HingedDoor`** (`objects/components/hinged_door.gd`, a Usable: E opens or closes it, a pick or the lock's key opens it through `Lockable.unlocked_by_user`, closing does not relock; it turns its entity -100 degrees about Y in 0.5 s; saveable). The sliding `Door` was not reused: it is a doorway (navigation carve, attenuator, panel travel), not a small hinged leaf.
- **The Map** (merged from a sub-agent): each node has its kind's colour strip on its left edge, its icon in the kind colour and a status square in the light's colours (blinking cut off), from `NetworkRow.kind_color` and `light` (NetworkState); the Server kind is retired.
- **Level touches** (no relayout): the two access points moved 0.11 m to their walls (the disc's origin is its back); the warehouse camera overrides follow `Model/Pivot`; the siren's aim point moved to its box front (`Breakable`), and the baton test reaches the 3.2 m siren from 0.9 m back (closer, the baton's 0.2 m sweep meets the wall before the 9 cm deep box).
