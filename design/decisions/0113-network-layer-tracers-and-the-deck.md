# 113. Network layer, tracers and the deck: gates in the reach graph, accounts and access, a tethered deck with Map and Icebreaker

Status: Accepted (builds [ADR 0112](0112-hacking-reconciled-with-the-built-game.md)'s M12a network half; extends [ADR 0077](0077-devices-cables-and-remote-commands.md) and [ADR 0099](0099-network-map-layout-pan-zoom.md))

Date: 2026-11-11

## Context

ADR 0112 split M12 and settled the player-facing design of network hacking ([hacking.md](../game/hacking.md), the approved [mockup](../prototypes/hacking_screens.html)). M12a builds it in two parallel parts: the computers (Halcyon OS desktops, another branch) and this one, the network layer, tracers and the deck. Devices, cables and reach already existed (ADR 0075, 0077: `Device`, `DeviceGraph`), with one implicit network and no addresses. The two parts share a small contract: `DeviceAccount` / `DeviceAccounts` (this branch) and `Computer.open_desktop(viewer, user, remote)` / `desktop_closed` (the computers' branch).

## Decision

### Network model (components and data on devices)

- **Address, subnet, trust, port** are exports on `Device` (`address`, `subnet`, `trusted`, `deck_port`); `DeviceNetwork` (static) resolves them: a device without a subnet takes the subnet of a cabled device that names one (a switch, never a gateway), else the implicit `&""` of every level without network data, so existing levels behave as before.
- **Gateways**: a `Gateway` component makes its Device a gate in the reach graph. `DeviceGraph` gained per-node subnets and gates: traffic that enters a gate from one subnet and leaves into a node of another passes only when the gate's `Callable(from, to)` says so; leaving on the side it came from is free, a gate is always reached itself, and it sends freely. Gates decide per final destination, so `reachable()` checks each node of the physical component (networks are small; a graph without gates keeps the single breadth-first search). `Gateway.passes(source, destination)`: its ordered `rules` first (`GatewayRule`: from any / subnet / device / you, to any / subnet / device, allow or deny; the first match wins; saved for M12b's Firewall), else a device's own traffic passes (`default_allow`) and a user's ("you") only when that user has an account on the gateway. That one default makes discovery: what lies behind an unhacked gateway is out of the deck's reach, so it is not on the map; hacking it (an account) lets the deck's traffic through and the subnets behind it appear; a gateway inside them is hacked in turn (chained).
- **Sources**: `NetworkSource` says where traffic comes from: a device (its own traffic), a deck on a wall jack (a graph node of its own, `Device.DECK_NODE`, cabled to the jack, on its subnet, the jack's address), on a maintenance port (that device only, address `port:<address>`), plugged into a computer (the computer's node and address, but the user's rights at the gates), or a user at a computer bare-handed. `Device.graph(tree, source)` builds the gates for that source.
- **Trust**: a device with a `trusted` list obeys commands only from the devices on it (and from a session on itself: the deck with an account); without a list, any device that reaches it (M8). Incidents are reports, not commands, and ignore trust.
- **Power and breaking** cut reach as before (a down node is never crossed); seen devices stay on the deck's map, greyed.

### Accounts (the shared contract)

`DeviceAccounts` (any device): `accounts: Array[DeviceAccount]` (user, password, strength Weak / Medium / Strong, admin), `account()`, `try_login(user, password, by)` (bare-handed: the device's own address and location), `login_from(..., source)` (the deck's), `grant()`, `has_access()`, `set_password()` (everyone loses that account), saved. A right login gives the account (no trace); a wrong one adds the failed-login trace on the tracers watching the device; a down device or a blocked address refuses without trace. The Journal knows a login as the computers' scheme ([ADR 0114](0114-computers-halcyon-os.md)): one entry per login, code `code_id()` = "user:password" (`LoginCredentials`), where `code_id()` is the component's `credentials_id`, else its Computer's, else the entity's name in snake_case; `known_login()` takes the latest learned for that user (the computers' "Fill in" and the Map's "Log in" read the same codes).

### Tracers

`Tracer` is an app component on a host (computer, server, gateway) watching named subnets, tuned by `TracerConfig` (`tracer.tres`: 4 %/s while a tool runs, +15 % failed login, +10 % subnet scan, drain 10 %/s from 2 s after the last gain, 30 s lockout; `tracer_sensitive.tres` 7 %/s). Pure logic in `TraceModel` (a trace per user, lockouts per address). It watches a device while it runs, its host is up and not disabled, and its host reaches the device. Every gain is multiplied by the user's `deck_trace_gain_mult`. Full trace: the host reports an incident at the user's entry point (`NetworkSource.entry_point()`) to the controllers it reaches, and blocks the address for the lockout: watched devices refuse it (`Tracer.blocks()`), the deck's tools against them stop, Icebreaker progress stays. Systems reach it through static helpers (`report()`, `report_running()`, `report_subnet()`, `blocks()`, `bars_for()`), found by group, like `Device.all()`.

### The deck

- **Item** `items/deck/deck.tres` (2 x 1, EQUIP_TOOL, `tool_kind` `deck`), in the catalogue, on the hub's loadout table and the inventory bay's item table.
- **Plugging**: `ToolUse` now works any tool target (a child with `accepts_tool` / `tool_hold` / `tool_release`), not only Locks; `Device` is the deck's target: LMB with the deck in hand on a device whose port (`DeviceNetwork.port_kind()`) is a JACK (explicit), a computer (HOST: a `Computer` component) or autonomous (MAINTENANCE: no cables) plugs the held deck in and raises its screen (`DeckSession.plug_and_raise()`).
- **`DeckSession`** (component on the player, not the screen) holds the plug, the source, discovery and access: tethered (more than 3 m horizontally from the plugged entity, unable to act or the deck gone: unplugged, tools stopped); discovery every 0.25 s: devices reached plus the dead ones behind (`DeviceGraph.connected()`), shown once their subnet is scanned (2 s, +10 % on the tracers watching it, once per subnet), gateways at once; seen devices and scanned subnets are kept in the deck stack's `state` (saved with the inventory, travelling with the item). `log_in()` with a Journal login, `send()` a command as the device's account holder (a CONTACT command stimulus from the user, logged `device.command` from the entry point), `open_remote()` a computer's desktop (`Computer.open_desktop(user, account, true)`, back to the map on `desktop_closed`).
- **`IcebreakerTool`** (beside it) runs the pure `Icebreaker`: progress += delta / (base time x `deck_crack_time_mult`), letters lock in one by one, only passwords up to `deck_max_strength`; trace per second on the watching tracers while it runs; progress kept on the deck per account (`state.cracks`, with a password hash: a changed password starts over); a finished crack grants the account and adds a PersonalLog entry carrying the login (the Journal lists it).
- **Computer skill** (skill table): `deck_crack_time_mult` x1 / 0.75 / 0.5 / 0.3, `deck_trace_gain_mult` x1 / 0.8 / 0.6 / 0.4, `deck_max_strength` 0 / 1 / 2 / 2, `deck_assist` 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 (the inventory's item details show them on the deck through its `deck_` prefix).
- **`DeckScreen`** (CanvasLayer on the player, built in code like the terminal): the mockup's deck, 85 % panel over the dimmed running world, input off, mouse free, HUD hidden; Esc (taken first: the game menu never opens) or ✕ lowers it, the deck stays plugged in and its tools keep running; LMB on the port raises it again. Title bar with entry point, address and one trace bar per tracer (`Tracer.bars_for()`, "unknown tracer" until its host is on the map); tools (Map, Icebreaker; Exploits, Firewall, Logs greyed: M12b); the Map is the moved `NetworkGraph` with the deck's rows (`NetworkRow`: address on the status line, amber with an account) and a `menu_builder` filling the device menu: address and status, accounts (access / "Log in (from <note>)" / Icebreaker with saved progress / "too strong"), a gateway's note, "Open its desktop (remote)", the device's commands with an account, else who it trusts. Assists: Trained crack time per account and time left; Advanced + "about +N % trace"; Master + "routes to N hidden subnets" on an unhacked gateway.
- **Map files** moved from `ui/terminal/` to `ui/deck/` (`network_graph.gd`, `network_layout.gd`, `network_view.gd`, their tests and fixtures). The graph no longer depends on the terminal: rows are `NetworkRow`, colours come from `DeckStyle`, command labels from a Callable. After the computers' merge the legacy terminal remains (`objects/terminal/`, `ui/terminal/terminal_style.gd`) were deleted: the map's readability checks (`tests/ui/deck/network_map_checks.gd`) build their own rows (`rows_from()`, `cables()`).

### Panic buttons and radio base stations

A generic `DeviceSwitch` component takes `command.disable` / `enable` for devices without a switch of their own: status words enabled / disabled, `device_disabled()`, saved. Panic buttons report nothing and radio base stations ignore broadcasts while off (their scenes gained the component and the two receptrons).

## Alternatives considered

- **A per-subnet visibility flag instead of gates**: the map would need its own rules for what is hidden; with gates, discovery is reach, and power cuts, rules and lockouts all follow from one graph.
- **Gate decisions per hop**: cheaper (one search), but rules name the final destination ("deny you to the console"), so a hop decision would be wrong on chained gateways.
- **Deck logic in the screen** (like the lockpicking screen's model): tools must keep running while the screen is lowered, so the session and the Icebreaker are components and the screen only shows them.
- **One `Tracer` trace for everyone**: per user (keyed by node name) costs nothing and keeps a second intruder (M16) apart.
- **The 2 s scan skipped**: kept, so plugging in has a moment of "scanning" and a scan costs trace as the tuning table says.

## Consequences

- Levels without network data are unchanged (tests: a cabled network with no subnet behaves as M8; the gallery and warehouse suites pass). The warehouse's office wall jack is a network switch without `deck_port = JACK`: the level brief that builds the warehouse network sets it.
- **Integration with the computers** (merged after ADR 0114): `Computer.controls()` is "on its cabled network and trusting it" (`DeviceNetwork.trusts()`), so a computer's Security app lists only devices that trust it; `Device.device_address()` gives the title bar its address; a remote desktop opens in the player's `ComputerScreen` over the deck's content area (`set_remote_area(DeckScreen.content_anchors())`), its Esc and "Back to the map" close it (the deck leaves that Esc alone) and `desktop_closed` returns the deck to its Map; `DeckScreen` fills the overlay's trace slot (`add_trace_bar()`) for remote desktops (the deck's tracers, compact and unnamed: the deck's own bar right above names them, and the desktop must fit the deck's content area) and bare-handed use (the tracers watching that computer for its address). `Device.connected_devices()` went (unused; `NetworkSource.connected()` does it for a source).
- **Commands that apply** (both the Security app and the deck's menu): `DeviceStatus.applicable()` offers, by the device's status words and disabled flag only, Enable alone while it is switched off, else everything but Enable, and of Stop / Resume sweep, Lock / Unlock, Open / Close the one that changes something.
- Cross-contract test: `tests/ui/deck/deck_computer_test.gd` (a remote desktop from the Map commanding a trusting camera, Back to the map and Esc; a failed bare-handed login raising the tracer whose bar shows in the computer overlay; a device trusting another computer not listed).
- Tests: `tests/objects/devices/device_graph_gates_test.gd` (unit), `tests/objects/network/` (`network_reach_test`, `device_accounts_test`, `trace_model_test`, `tracer_test`; minimal entities from `network_fixtures.gd`), `tests/items/deck/` (`icebreaker_test`: measured crack times per tier against the design table, determinism; `deck_session_test`: discovery, chain, seen devices, cracks, resume, password change, lockout, tether, maintenance port, commands, remote desktop), `tests/ui/deck/` (`deck_screen_test`, `deck_map_test`: the real player in `fixtures/deck_lab.tscn`). ## Amendment: the Map draws the plugged network only (2026-11-11)

Playtest bug: the deck drew every device it had ever seen, wherever it was plugged in (the network lab's map in the security wing, two networks with no connection). The deck still remembers everything it has seen (`seen` in the stack's state is never cleared), but `DeckSession.map_devices()` now keeps only the seen devices on the **physical network of the entry point**: `DeviceNetwork.physical_network(device)`, the device plus everything cables and holding wireless links join it to, both ways, up or down, through routers whatever their rules (`DeviceGraph.component()` of `Device.graph()`). The entry point is the jack on a wall jack, the access point when joined over Wi-Fi, the device itself on a maintenance port (its whole network is drawn; the port still limits what the deck reaches) or a computer. Discovery is unchanged on top of it: scanned subnets only, nothing behind an unhacked router. Unplugged, the Map is empty and says "Plug the deck into a device to see its network."; its button stays enabled for that (Exploits and Firewall stay disabled). Tests: `tests/objects/network/physical_network_test.gd`, `tests/items/deck/deck_map_scope_test.gd`, `tests/ui/deck/deck_map_scope_test.gd` (a second network: `network_fixtures.gd annex()`). Review shot: `deck_shots.tscn` `SHOT=map_second`.
