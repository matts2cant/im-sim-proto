# 122. Network readability: status lights, gear plates, the deck's AR cable view, the drop line

Status: Accepted (builds the readability half of [ADR 0115](0115-m12b-intrusion-and-m12c-consequences.md)'s M12c)

Date: 2026-11-12

## Context

ADR 0115 settled that the network must read in the world, not only on the deck's Map: one LED colour language on every device, plates on network gear naming what it feeds, closets with one look, an AR view of the cables generated from the link data (no cables modelled in levels), and a HUD line after an action that takes devices off the network. This ADR records how they are built and the rules they follow.

## Decision

- **Status lights** (`StatusLight`, `objects/devices/status_light.gd`): every `Device` attaches one to its entity (deferred from `Device._ready`, after the level's cables are in) when it is on a network: a cable either way, a holding wireless link or wireless clients, or an access point named. A standalone device (no cables: a maintenance port) shows none; `Device.status_light` off opts out. Colours: green steady up and on its network; amber blinking powered but cut off (none of the network gear it links to is up; linked to no gear: none of its links); red broken but powered, or switched off by a command (`DeviceStatus.is_disabled()`); dark unpowered. Network gear (`DeviceNetwork.is_network_gear()`) also shows one port light per cabled link (8, then "+"), green while the gear and the far end are up. Placement: optional `StatusLedMarker` / `PortLightsMarker` children (the laser array and access point have them), else the top-front of the entity's visible bounds (+Z is the front). Refreshed only on signals (the device's and its links' state, power, breakage and commands); the blink is a shader with per-instance parameters (one shared material). The gear scenes lost their decorative indicator strips.
- **Gear plates** (`GearPlate`, `objects/network/gear_plate.gd`): a Label3D on network gear generated from the links, "SW-VAULT · feeds DOOR-VAULT, TUR-VAULT, CAM-OFFICE" (4 names, then "+N"), shown on two lines under the gear on the wall plane (lower under deep gear). A router feeds all its links; other gear all but the routers (its uplink). Names are the screens' names (`NetworkSource.display_name()`).
- **Closets** share one kit part, `levels/kit/network_panel.tscn` (`NetworkPanel`): a painted wall panel with a stripe in its subnet's Map colour and "NETWORK" on it, visual only, 2 mm off the wall. Placed behind the warehouse's basement network closet and the security wing's network wall.
- **The deck's AR cable view** (`DeckArView`, `items/deck/deck_ar_view.gd`, a Player component): with the deck in hand, RMB (`alt_fire`) toggles it; putting the deck away, being unable to act or opening any screen (the player's input off) turns it off; the world keeps running and the HUD shows "AR: cables (RMB)". It draws the links between devices the held deck has seen (its stack's `seen` list, both ends seen) within 30 m (either end), at most 200: cables as ceiling runs (up, across under the ceiling over the end nearer the player, a ray up else 3 m, down) in the subnet's colour, wireless links as dashed light-blue lines, down links dim, no depth test. Rebuilt on toggle, on the deck's map changes, on a drawn device's state change and every 4 m the player moves.
- **The drop line** (`NetworkFeedback` on the HUD, `NetworkDrops` for the logic): when a device goes down and takes others off the network, the HUD message line shows "SW-VAULT down: 5 devices offline (CAM-OFFICE, DOOR-VAULT, +3)" for 4 s. Offline: the up devices joined through it split into pieces once it is down (cables and wireless links, routers' rules ignored); the one largest piece of two or more stays the network, the others are offline. Only for drops within 30 m of the player or caused by the player (MissionTally's attribution of breakage, or a breaker the player switched off in the same tick). The level's starting state (first frames) never shows a line.

## Consequences

- No per-level work: a new networked device or a new level gets lights, plates and AR runs from its link data. Placement markers are optional.
- The LED's amber rule is local (its own links) while the drop line counts whole pieces: a router going down blinks only the switches behind it but the line names every device behind them.
- The AR runs cross floors and walls (no depth test, the brief's "drawn on top"); runs between floors rise on the player's side.
- Physics cost: none per frame (signal-driven); measured in the warehouse within noise (see the M12c report).

## Amendment (M13 playtest fix): lights on the surface, riding with their part

Playtest: LEDs floated in the air on cameras, sirens, computers, radio base stations, access points, panic buttons and turrets, and sat off the leaf of doors. The top-front corner of the visible bounds is only on the device when the device is a box: a protrusion (camera head and lens, siren horn, computer shelf, panic button, wall jack socket) pushed the front plane out, a tall thin part (radio and access point antennas) pushed the top up, the turret's laser beam (visible when the light attached) pushed the front 1 m out, and the door's handle pushed the left edge past the leaf. The leaf and the turret head also move, while the light stayed put.

- Placement is now: a `StatusLedMarker` / `PortLightsMarker` anywhere under the entity (found by name), else the surface of its visible meshes (`DeviceSurface`, `objects/devices/device_surface.gd`): rays along -Z from in front of the entity, rows from the top and each row from the left, take the first spot where the LED's 32 mm square lands on one mesh, on a face turned at most about 45 degrees from the front, flat within 4 mm; the LED goes 3 mm proud along that face's normal. Port lights go on the surface 35 mm below the LED, centred. Only without any such spot does the old bounds corner remain.
- Each light is a child of the node it sits on (the marker's parent, or the mesh it was projected on), so it moves and hides with that part; the StatusLight frees them with itself.
- Data where the shape has no good automatic spot: the turret has a marker on its static base (its head turns); the door's marker is a child of its leaf, and `Door` places it at the top-left of the part of the leaf that shows in the doorway (the rim hides in the jamb), on a barred leaf's top rail.
- Test: `tests/objects/devices/status_light_placement_test.gd` cables every device scene under `objects/` and checks each light's centre and back corners lie within 1 cm of a visible mesh and face the front; minimal entities show the rule skips protrusions and antennas and rides a moving part.

## Amendment (2026-11-12, one light)

Playtest: "Some devices now have 2 light indicators, which is very confusing. We should limit to 1 light." The LED was added on top of lights the devices already had: the camera's watch lamp, the lock light on a door's wall boxes, the decorative glowing indicators of the alarm controller, panic button, radio base station, siren and mail server, and (seen in the review shots) the turret's red sight and the laser emitter's red lens.

- **One light per device, one vocabulary.** A new component, `DeviceLamp` (`objects/devices/device_lamp.gd`), is a device's own lamp: lamp meshes from exported paths or registered in code (`set_meshes()`), the owner's colour from `show_own(colour, lit)`. When the entity has a DeviceLamp with a lamp mesh, its `StatusLight` adds no LED and sends its state to the lamp (`show_network()`), which shows, by priority: dark (unpowered, switched off by a command, or broken: `DARK` / `DISABLED`); blinking amber (cut off; the only blinking state); otherwise the owner's colours. Without a StatusLight (no network) the lamp shows the owner's colour alone. A lamp that gains or loses its meshes later (a door's hardware rebuilt for a lock added in code) swaps the LED out or in. Lamps paint with the LED's shader (`status_light_material.tres`, per-instance colour and blink), so they blink exactly like an LED; repainted only on a change.
- **Who has a lamp** (data, not code): the security camera scene declares one on its `Pivot/Tilt/Lamp` (`SecurityCamera` sets green / amber / red, unlit while blind); the door scene declares an empty one and `DoorHardware` registers its wall boxes' `Light` meshes (both faces show the same state: one light per side), painted by `Door` (red locked, green unlocked or open, dark broken or unpowered). A door without wall boxes keeps the LED on its leaf. Any future device with a lamp needs only a DeviceLamp in its scene.
- **Every other device** keeps the LED (green, blinking amber, red, dark); network gear keeps its port lights.
- **Decorative lights stop glowing**: the indicators keep their shape with a plain dark lens material (`objects/devices/indicator_lens.tres`; `indicator_led.tres` is gone, and the unused LED sub-resources in the switch, router and access point scenes went with it); they are no longer `Powered` targets or hidden when broken (nothing to show). The turret's sight uses the same lens; the laser emitter's lens is dark glass (the beam itself still glows, and the lens still hides when broken).
- Test: `tests/objects/devices/device_lamp_test.gd` (minimal entities: no LED beside a lamp, owner colour up, amber blink cut off even over a red owner colour, dark unpowered or disabled, back with the link, LED swapped when the lamp gains or loses meshes; the shipped camera red while it sees a target and amber when cut off while still seeing it; a keypad door's two lock lights and no leaf LED, a bare door's leaf LED); `status_light_placement_test.gd` checks every device scene shows its lamp or the LED, never both, port lights only on gear, and no other glowing mesh under 8 cm.
