Device design language

One language for every device: a strip in the device kind's colour, the kind's Map icon, one status light (none on wall jacks, panic buttons and lasers), network ports visible with a label naming what each is cabled to (on network gear, computers, alarm controllers and wall jacks; small single-purpose devices hide theirs), and a name plate. Rack and standalone versions of the same device look like the same family. The deck's Map draws the same strip and icon.

1 · Every device, every version at the game's own pixel scale

Port labels run vertically under each socket (your choice a). Cameras, turrets, sirens, radio base stations, panic buttons, lasers and door controllers show no port; the wall jack keeps its socket and label. At the 2 m reach most labels are too small to read; lean in (0.5 m or closer) to read a switch's ports. The light on a door controller is its lock light (red locked, green unlocked) and also shows the network problems; the camera keeps its own lamp.

2 · Linking bays open, and locked glass door (lockpick or key)

3 · The deck's Map same strip and icon on every node

Each node: the kind's colour strip on its left edge, its icon in that colour, the name, and the status square (the light's colours; cut off blinks). Cables are solid lines, wireless links dashed. A computer's installed apps are not on the node; they show in its device menu. Rack and desk versions of a device look the same on the Map: it is a map of the network, not of the rooms.

Key added to the keychain: Linking bay A

Keys are picked up into the keychain, never into the grid: they take no space and cannot be dropped or thrown. A lock whose key you hold opens with E; without it, pick the lock. The frame on a locked thing reads "Locked · key or lockpick".
